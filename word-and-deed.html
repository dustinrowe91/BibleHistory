The full register

How the count was made

  1. One event, counted once. When the same event appears in more than one Gospel, the accounts are merged and the parallels listed. The event gets credit for everything any account records. So if Luke adds a healing that Mark leaves out, the event counts as both.
  2. Word means preaching, teaching, an evangelistic conversation, or a commission to go and proclaim. A one-line word that only performs or dismisses a healing ("Be clean," "Your faith has made you well") does not count as teaching.
  3. Deed means an act that meets someone's need: healing, casting out a demon, raising the dead, feeding, provision, rescue, service, or giving. Signs with no one helped (the fig tree, the coin in the fish, tongues, the viper) and acts of judgment (Elymas, Ananias and Sapphira) are not counted.
  4. Together means both happen in the same scene or in one continuous narrative unit, not just in neighboring chapters.
  5. Who is Jesus or his followers: the Twelve, the Seventy, Stephen, Philip, Ananias, Paul and his companions, and the churches. John the Baptist is not included.
  6. Summary statements such as Matthew 4:23 ("teaching, preaching, healing") describe many unrecorded episodes at once. They are shown outlined and kept out of the headline figures.
  7. Left out: the birth narratives, the baptism and temptation, lists of the Twelve, plots and betrayals, the prayer in Gethsemane, trials where Jesus is silent, burial, travel notes, Peter's escape from prison, the Ephesian riot, and Paul's legal plea before Festus.
  8. Where it can't be scored cleanly, the entry carries a judgment-call note saying how it was scored. Two passages missing from the earliest manuscripts, John 7:53-8:11 and Mark 16:9-20, are noted. Mark 16 is used only as a parallel.

One caution about the second figure: teaching scenes come in finer pieces than miracles, since the Gospels split discourse into many short exchanges. So the share of good works that come with a word is the steadier number. The share of teaching that comes with a good work depends more on where the episode lines are drawn.

What the pattern teaches

Ground and fruit
Ephesians 2:8-10 excludes works as the ground of salvation and includes them as its purpose. Faith alone justifies, but the faith that justifies is never alone. The register shows the same order. The word is constant, and mercy follows close behind it.
Signs and mercy
Jesus's miracles identify him (John 20:30-31) and are also real compassion. In the register, his healings are paired with teaching about as often as his followers' are.
Priority without separation
Acts 6 sets apart the Seven to care for widows so the apostles can give themselves to the word and prayer. The roles are distinguished, and neither is dropped. The word-only stretches of Acts sit alongside a church that shares its goods and sends famine relief.

Read and checked against the King James Version text of Matthew, Mark, Luke, John and Acts. Every reference and parallel was verified to exist in that text. The classifications are a careful reader's judgment, and each borderline case is marked.
