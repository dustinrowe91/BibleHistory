Matthew 1 and Luke 3

The Line of Jesus

Jesus' family line from Adam to Joseph, following both Gospel genealogies. Where Matthew and Luke trace different sons, the line splits into two columns, then joins again where they meet. Tap anyone for who they were and where to read about them.

Why two genealogies?

Different purposes
Matthew writes "the son of David, the son of Abraham" and runs forward from Abraham through Solomon and the kings of Judah, showing Jesus as the rightful heir to David's throne. Luke runs backward from Jesus to "Adam, the son of God," showing Him as the Savior of all humanity.
Two lines after David
Matthew follows Solomon; Luke follows Nathan, another son of David and Bathsheba. The two meet at Shealtiel and Zerubbabel, then part again until Joseph. Two explanations have long been given. Many hold that Luke traces Mary's line, with Heli as her father and Joseph counted as his son by marriage. Others, since Julius Africanus around AD 220, hold that both lines are Joseph's: one by birth and one by law, through a levirate marriage or an adoption.
Matthew's fourteens
Matthew arranges the names in three sets of fourteen (1:17): Abraham to David, David to the exile, and the exile to Christ. To do this he passes over some kings, such as Ahaziah, Joash, and Amaziah between Joram and Uzziah. In Hebrew usage "the father of" can mean "the ancestor of."
Women in Matthew
Matthew names four women besides Mary: Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, and "the wife of Uriah," Bathsheba. Several were foreigners, and each has a story of scandal or grace, a sign of the kind of people the Messiah came to save.
Joseph and Jesus
Both Gospels stop short of calling Joseph Jesus' father. Matthew says Joseph was "the husband of Mary, of whom Jesus was born," and Luke that Jesus was "the son (as was supposed) of Joseph." Through Joseph, Jesus is David's legal heir. Through Mary, He was born of a virgin.
Names follow the ESV of Matthew 1:1–17 and Luke 3:23–38. Where Genesis or Kings spells a name differently, both forms are given. The names from Adam to Abraham appear only in Luke, and Genesis 5 and 11 give the life spans. Kings link to the Kings & Prophets page, and key events to the Timeline.
