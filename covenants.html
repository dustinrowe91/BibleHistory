The Covenants

God binds Himself to His people by covenant. Each covenant builds on the one before it, and every one of them is fulfilled in Christ. Follow them in order, or pick one promise and trace it from Adam to the New Covenant.

How the covenants fit together

One promise, unfolding
The covenants are not separate plans but one purpose of God, revealed step by step. The law given at Sinai did not cancel the promise made to Abraham 430 years before. The saints of old "died in faith, not having received the things promised," which were kept for Christ. Galatians 3:15–18 · Hebrews 11:13, 39–40
Works and grace
Adam was promised life for obedience, and failed. Every covenant after the fall rests on God's grace, received by faith, and points to the one Man who would obey in our place. "As by the one man's disobedience the many were made sinners, so by the one man's obedience the many will be made righteous." Romans 5:18–19 · Galatians 3:21–22
Shadow and substance
The law, the tabernacle, the priests, and the sacrifices were "a shadow of the good things to come." Christ did not come to abolish them but to fulfill them, and the New Covenant is better because its mediator, sacrifice, and promises are better. Hebrews 8:5–6; 10:1 · Matthew 5:17
Signs old and new
Each covenant has its sign. Circumcision marked Abraham's family, and baptism now marks those buried and raised with Christ. The Passover lamb gave way to the Lord's Supper, where Christ our Passover is remembered until He comes. Colossians 2:11–12 · 1 Corinthians 5:7; 11:23–26
"Yes" in Christ
"All the promises of God find their Yes in him." Offspring, land, blessing, throne, and heart all come to rest in one Person, and through Him to everyone who belongs to Him. 2 Corinthians 1:20 · Galatians 3:29
Scripture quotations follow the ESV. Dates match the Timeline's conservative chronology. The covenant with Adam has no "covenant" word in Genesis 2, but Hosea 6:7 speaks of it as one, and Paul sets Adam beside Christ as the head of humanity (Romans 5).
