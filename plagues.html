The Plagues of Egypt

Ten plagues, with the sign that opened the contest and the sea that ended it: twelve judgments on Pharaoh and the gods of Egypt. Each one shows who is the LORD, and each echoes on through Scripture, to the cross and the last judgment. "On all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments: I am the LORD" (Exodus 12:12).

The pattern: a sign, three rounds of three, the firstborn, and the sea

Highlight

The hardening of Pharaoh's heart

Before Moses ever went to Egypt, God told him, "I will harden his heart, so that he will not let the people go" (Exodus 4:21; 7:3). Then, plague by plague, the text says three things: Pharaoh hardened his heart, his heart was hardened, and the LORD hardened his heart. Through the first five plagues Pharaoh hardens himself or "is hardened." From the sixth on, the LORD is named as the one who hardens him, though at the hail Pharaoh "hardened his heart" once more (9:34).

Pharaoh hardened his own heart"His heart was hardened"The LORD hardened his heart

Both are true together. God is sovereign over the heart of a king (Proverbs 21:1), and Pharaoh is fully guilty for his own pride and cruelty. Paul draws the lesson in Romans 9: "For this very purpose I have raised you up, that I might show my power in you, and that my name might be proclaimed in all the earth. So then he has mercy on whomever he wills, and he hardens whomever he wills." When the objector asks how God can still find fault, Paul does not soften either truth, but answers, "Who are you, O man, to answer back to God?" Romans 9:14–24

The warning stands for every hearer. The Philistines remembered: "Why should you harden your hearts as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts?" And Hebrews still says, "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts." 1 Samuel 6:6 · Hebrews 3:7–15

"That you may know that I am the LORD"

The plagues are not random disasters. God says again and again why He sends them: so that Egypt, Pharaoh, and Israel would know who He is. He is the LORD, there is none like Him in all the earth, the earth is His, and He makes a distinction between His people and the world.

The plagues and the last judgment

Revelation's trumpets and bowls deliberately echo Egypt. The judgments that fell on one kingdom foreshadow the judgment that will fall on the whole world in rebellion against God. And the people of God, delivered through it all, stand beside a sea and sing "the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb" (Revelation 15:3).

The two witnesses of Revelation 11 likewise have "power over the waters to turn them into blood and to strike the earth with every kind of plague." Revelation 11:6

Redeemed by blood

In the first plagues God set Israel apart without any sign. At the last plague He did not. Israel was spared not because they were better than Egypt, but because a lamb died in the place of their firstborn and its blood was on the door. "When I see the blood, I will pass over you." Every household under the blood was safe, and every household without it was not.

That night is the gospel in picture. "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed." He was killed at Passover, not a bone of Him was broken, and by His blood God's people are redeemed, "not with perishable things such as silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish or spot." The judgment that passed over Israel fell on Him: darkness over the land at noon, and the death of God's only Son, so that all who shelter under His blood are passed over. Exodus 12:13 · 1 Corinthians 5:7 · 1 Peter 1:18–19 · John 19:36

Quotations follow the ESV. Scripture does not number the plagues; Exodus records ten, and Psalms 78 and 105 retell them in their own order and selection. The sign of the staff and the drowning at the sea are counted here because they open and close the same contest with Pharaoh. The Egyptian gods each plague is "often linked with" are scholarly suggestions, not claims the text makes. The text says only that God judged "all the gods of Egypt" (Exodus 12:12; Numbers 33:4).
